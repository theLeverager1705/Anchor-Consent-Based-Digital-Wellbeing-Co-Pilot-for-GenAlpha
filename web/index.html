The solution

Two apps, one honest loop

One Android app with two experiences. The kid is a first-class user, not a monitoring target. The product only works if kids want to open it.

Kid Mode

A Sprout that grows with honest check-ins, not happy ones

  • Vibe Check: a 15-second emoji check-in with an optional camera mirror that is never analyzed
  • Private journal with on-device sentiment. Only shared if the child taps "share"
  • Transition Check: one tap ~75 seconds after a screen session ends
  • "Something bothered me": tell family in two taps, no trouble
  • Anchor Time: phone-free dinner, homework or outdoor timers
  • "What my family can see": a live mirror of the parent view, plus sharing toggles and a pause button
Parent Mode

Pattern-level visibility, written as a conversation

  • Family Digest with an optional Claude-written summary from aggregated numbers only
  • "Worth a conversation?" alerts, each with a conversation starter
  • 20-day trends: mood, category mix, transition reactions, weekly screen time
  • App Lens: what TikTok, Roblox, Snapchat etc. are, age ratings, settings to check together
  • Family goals the child agrees to. Anchor never blocks apps
  • Gen Z guides: older siblings or mentors can join the circle

Why Transition Check

The meltdown when the tablet goes away is a signal, not defiance

Research on device-removal reactions describes a withdrawal response (dopamine crash, time-blindness, loss of control). Instead of filming a child's face, Anchor measures the moment after, with the child's own tap.

Session ends

Android's Usage Access API notices a long session in a video, game or social app has ended. No screen contents are read.

~75 seconds later

A gentle notification: "Quick check: how do you feel right now?" No camera, no mic.

One tap

The child picks an emoji; reaction time and intensity are recorded as derived scores only.

A pattern, not a verdict

3 of the last 5 tough transitions → "worth a conversation", with a starter like trying a 2-minute warning.

The non-negotiables

Privacy & trust by design

Built to COPPA (2025), BIPA-style and GDPR-K / UK Children's Code principles from day one, and to Google Play's rules for transparent child-monitoring apps.

What Anchor never does

  • Background or covert camera / microphone access
  • Face-reading models or any claim to diagnose a condition
  • Read messages, photos, keystrokes or what's on screen
  • Upload raw video, audio or private journal text
  • Block apps or hide from the child

What Anchor always does

  • Kid-friendly consent with a press-and-hold "agree together"
  • Persistent "Anchor is on" notification on the child's phone
  • Child-controlled sharing toggles and a visible pause
  • Syncs only derived summaries; AI sees aggregated numbers only
  • Frames every alert as "worth a conversation", with real helplines
